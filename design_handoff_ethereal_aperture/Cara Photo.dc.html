Ethereal Aperture

ethereal adj.
extremely delicate, light, not of this world.
Photography is fleeting. The light is there for a second and then it isn't, and some of it will never come back the same way.
aperture n.
an opening, hole, or gap; the opening in a lens that admits light.
The photographs are openings of their own — into what we noticed, what we were willing to look at, and what we made from it.

And I really love alliteration.

Experiments, teaching, mistakes, darkrooms, and other things I find by looking closely.

02 My work Some things I've seen through the lens

Current work · ongoing · changes often

I'm less interested in a perfect image than in what happens when the process slips, doubles back, or makes its own decisions.

Most of what I make now starts as a mistake I decide to keep. I re-develop old prints, leave film in the wrong places, and let the chemistry work longer than it should.

03 About me

Draft copy — replace with your own words

In the darkroom, Lawrenceville, 2026

I teach classes where the camera is allowed to misbehave.

I started with a borrowed camera and no idea what I was doing, which turned out to be the right way in. A friend showed me how to develop a roll in a kitchen sink, and the part that stayed with me wasn't the picture — it was watching something appear that I had only partly controlled. I've worked in analog ever since, mostly black-and-white, mostly at home or in shared darkrooms.

Now I re-develop old prints, shoot film long past its date, and make photographs without a camera at all. The work is slow and a fair amount of it fails. I keep the failures that are more interesting than what I intended.

I teach in and around Pittsburgh — darkroom evenings in Lawrenceville, weekend workshops in Braddock, and occasional sessions at community spaces in the region. Classes are small. We start with what the process actually does rather than with rules, and everyone prints. If you've never touched film, that's a fine place to begin; if you've been printing for years, there's usually something in the class that misbehaves in a new way.

My photographs have appeared in the Halide Project publication, at an analog photography festival in Pittsburgh, and in regional group shows. I'd rather the classes and the student work speak for the teaching, so I keep this part short.
